#+title: Fix audio playback on nixOS
#+author: piotr-yuxuan

Directions for the sound problems of nixOS that the configuration
leaves to its owner. [[file:../explanation/nixOS-audio.org][Audio on nixOS]] explains the choices behind
them. Devices are named by role: the Bluetooth speaker, the LDAC
headphones, the USB webcam's microphone and the laptop speakers.

The directions assume that you logged out and in, while no music
played, after the switch that applied this configuration: WirePlumber
reads its settings and scripts when it starts. They were written from
the configuration and from a diagnosis on nixOS, without being run,
since writing this page could not play sound or change the audio
state. The steps in the BIOS cannot be run from Linux. If a step
behaves differently, write down what you saw so that this page can be
corrected.

* Choose the default microphone

1. In GNOME Settings > Sound, under Input, choose the USB webcam's
   microphone.
2. Close Settings: while the panel shows, its input level meter keeps
   the microphone open.
3. In Chromium and in Signal, choose the default microphone or the
   webcam's in the application's own settings, so that no call opens
   the microphone of a Bluetooth device.

Never save =bluetooth.autoswitch-to-headset-profile= with
=wpctl settings --save=: a saved value overrides the configuration and
brings automatic call mode back.

Check:

#+begin_src sh
wpctl inspect @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SOURCE@ | grep node.name
wpctl settings bluetooth.autoswitch-to-headset-profile
#+end_src

The first command names the webcam's microphone, =alsa_input.usb-...=;
the second reports =false=.

* Use the headphones' microphone in a call

Call mode never starts by itself: choose it before the call and leave
it after.

1. Turn the volume down: call mode keeps a volume of its own, which can
   be much louder than the music's.
2. In GNOME Settings > Sound, under Output, choose the headphones, then
   their Headset configuration (Headset Head Unit, HSP/HFP). The music
   output goes away, so a player with media controls pauses.
3. In the call application, choose the headphones' microphone.
4. After the call, choose the High Fidelity Playback configuration
   (A2DP Sink) again, and the webcam's microphone if the call
   application changed the default.

Check, during the call and after it:

#+begin_src sh
wpctl inspect @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SINK@ | grep -E 'api\.bluez5\.(profile|codec)'
#+end_src

During the call the profile reads =headset-head-unit=; after it,
=a2dp-sink=, with the codec =ldac=.

* Music sounds like a phone call

Music that turns mono and muffled, or suddenly louder, on a Bluetooth
output means that the device is in call mode. Read its profile and the
setting that would switch it by itself:

#+begin_src sh
wpctl inspect @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SINK@ | grep -E 'api\.bluez5\.(profile|codec)'
wpctl settings bluetooth.autoswitch-to-headset-profile
#+end_src

If the profile reads =headset-head-unit=:

1. Turn the volume down.
2. In GNOME Settings > Sound, under Output, choose the High Fidelity
   Playback configuration.

If the device goes back to call mode whenever an application opens a
microphone, and the setting reports =true=, a saved value overrides
the configuration. Delete it, then log out and in, while no music
plays:

#+begin_src sh
wpctl settings --delete bluetooth.autoswitch-to-headset-profile
#+end_src

If the setting still reports =true= after that, the running system
does not have this configuration yet.

Check: the first command above reads =a2dp-sink=, with the codec =aac=
on the speaker or =ldac= on the headphones.

* Bluetooth stays off after resume

systemd-rfkill saves the state of each radio and restores it when the
adapter comes back, as it does after hibernation. Bluetooth that was
switched off before, by its toggle, by airplane mode or by an
application, therefore comes back off.

1. Look at the Bluetooth toggle in GNOME's quick settings, or run:
   #+begin_src sh
   rfkill list bluetooth
   #+end_src
   =Soft blocked: yes= means that Bluetooth was switched off.
2. Turn Bluetooth on with the toggle, or with
   =rfkill unblock bluetooth=.

Check: =rfkill list bluetooth= shows =Soft blocked: no=, and GNOME
Settings > Bluetooth lists the devices, which connect again when they
are switched on.

* Music continues on the laptop speakers

When a Bluetooth output goes away, WirePlumber pauses the players that
publish media controls (MPRIS), sandboxed or not, such as VLC, Firefox
and mpv. Other players, such as =ffplay= or a terminal player, move to
the laptop speakers at their own volume, and a pause that fails is not
tried again.

1. Pause or stop the player.
2. Choose the output in GNOME's quick settings, or in GNOME Settings >
   Sound, under Output.
3. Play again.

To know whether a player publishes media controls, list them while it
plays; a player missing from the list is never paused for you:

#+begin_src sh
busctl --user list | grep org.mpris.MediaPlayer2
#+end_src

Check: once a Bluetooth output went away, a player that publishes
media controls reads =Paused=. Use the name that the list shows, here
VLC's:

#+begin_src sh
busctl --user get-property org.mpris.MediaPlayer2.vlc \
  /org/mpris/MediaPlayer2 org.mpris.MediaPlayer2.Player PlaybackStatus
#+end_src

After step 2, the default output is the one you chose, for a Bluetooth
output =bluez_output.XX_XX_XX_XX_XX_XX.1=:

#+begin_src sh
wpctl inspect @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SINK@ | grep node.name
#+end_src

* Apply Framework's speaker EQ in the BIOS

The setting Linux Audio Compatibility makes the firmware apply
Framework's own EQ to the laptop speakers when it reads Linux. These
steps run in the firmware's Setup Utility, so they cannot be run from
Linux, and they were not run when this page was written.

1. Restart into the Setup Utility:
   #+begin_src sh
   systemctl reboot --firmware-setup
   #+end_src
   or press F2 at the Framework logo while the laptop starts.
2. Open Advanced > Linux Audio Compatibility. If it reads Windows, set
   it to Linux.
3. Save the change and exit.

Check: at the next visit to the Setup Utility, the setting reads Linux.
Then play a track you know well on the laptop speakers and compare by
ear.
